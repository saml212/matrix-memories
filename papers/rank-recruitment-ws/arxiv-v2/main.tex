\begin{abstract}
Can gradient-based training learn the rank needed to store and compose
associations in a matrix memory? A prior negative result used a
matrix-augmented reasoner on a task that admits a rank-1 solution,
leaving this question open. We train matrix memories on $K$ fresh key--value
bindings whose exact linear recovery requires $\rank(Z) \geq K$.
A fixed linear readout queries a single matrix state without access to
the original bindings. Experiments measure recovery by cosine similarity
greater than 0.9, a threshold distinct from mathematical equality.
Learned effective rank increases with $K$ across the tested grid
(Spearman $\rho = 1.0$ at $d = 16$).
% <!-- evidence: R1 -->
Training-time rank caps produce a recovery transition near $k = K$:
at $d = 8$, $K = 4$, rank 3 gives at most 0.0004 recovery and rank 4
gives 0.97.
% <!-- evidence: R2 -->
Four of five seeds retain at least 0.9996 recovery through 21-fold
self-application of the trained operator.
% <!-- evidence: R3 -->
On the entity subspace, the learned operator has effective rank close
to $K$ and approximates the ideal cycle.
% <!-- evidence: R5 -->
For the single converged seed capped below $K$, the eigenspectrum
predicts the measured cosine within 0.008 through seven applications.
% <!-- evidence: R4 -->
Extending training resolves several initial failures, but recovery still
declines at larger matrix dimensions with encoder width fixed.
% <!-- evidence: R8 -->
% <!-- evidence: R7 -->
\end{abstract}
